# Positioning

## The one line

Colony buys the facts behind a credit decision, prices them, and prints the ones it
could not buy.

## Who it is for

Treasury, credit and vendor-risk teams at companies that sell on terms. They already
have a policy, a spreadsheet and a PDF vendor. What they do not have is a decision
where the evidence is priced and the gaps are named.

## What it replaces

| Today | Colony |
|---|---|
| A $40/seat report refreshed quarterly | A $0.08 decision, refreshed on demand |
| "Sanctions: clear" with no source | A receipt per claim, and the exact list of what failed |
| A one-number risk score | A verdict plus the claim it hinges on |
| Trust me | Verify the hash |

## Why now

Telegraph makes a single fact cheap and ranked. That changes the unit of work: instead
of buying a report, you buy the eight facts the report was made of, and you buy the
ninth only when two of the first eight disagree.

## Against alternatives

- **Against a single-model agent:** one model will happily write "no sanctions concerns"
  from memory. Colony cannot say that unless a receipt says it.
- **Against a data vendor:** a vendor sells coverage. Colony sells a verdict with an
  audit trail and an explicit blind spot.
- **Against v2 (three answers to one question):** 3x the cost for a consistency score.
  The founder's critique was correct; the fix was to make depth conditional, not to
  remove it.

## The honest limits

- Agreement measures consistency, not truth. Three miners can share one bad source.
- Coverage is only as good as the intents the network serves today. Filings and news
  disagree often, and Colony shows that rather than smoothing it.
- Testnet pricing. The $0.08 figure is measured on the devnode, not a mainnet quote.
